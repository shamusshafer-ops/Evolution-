What am I looking at?

A natural-selection simulator. Organisms forage, spend energy, reproduce, and die. Nobody designs the outcome — the environment selects it.

Reading the screen

  • Specimen well (top) — organisms as coloured dots. Colour is emergent species (clade), largest population first. Species are not designed in; they're derived from who can still interbreed.
  • Census strip — population by species over time. A new coloured band appearing is a speciation event happening live.
  • Drift ribbon — each trait's distribution across the population, sliding through generations. A bright core migrating up or down a band is natural selection, made visible.

The traits

Four heritable traits. Three are priced by real biology, not numbers picked to feel balanced:

  • Speed — costs mass × speed² (kinetic)
  • Size — costs mass0.75, Kleiber's law: real metabolic scaling, observed across ~27 orders of magnitude of body mass
  • Sense — costs sense²; wide vision is neurally expensive tissue
  • Diet — which of two resources an organism digests; no metabolic cost, the price is opportunity, what it can't eat

How evolution happens here

Reproduction is sexual: two parents, real per-trait recombination (not a blend of the two — blending would erase the variance selection needs). Mutation adds small random changes on top. Nothing copies perfectly.

Species aren't chosen. Two organisms are the same species if they could still interbreed — close enough in trait space, and able to physically find each other. When a lineage drifts far enough — by diet, by geography, or sometimes just by chance — gene flow stops and a new species exists, detected the moment it happens, not scripted in advance.

Pressures worth trying

  • Plains vs Oasis — same total food, different distribution. Plains rewards speed; Oasis rewards sense.
  • Monoculture vs Oasis — identical except resource count. One resource: exclusion, a single species wins. Two: coexistence, specialists partition the two resources between them.
  • Archipelago — two resource clusters, an empty gap between them. Distance alone ends gene flow, no dietary preference required. Watch the census strip: two bands that stay geographically separated rather than intermixed — the signature of a different speciation mechanism than Oasis's.
  • Seasonal — food oscillates. Watch population peak heading into the lean phase, then crash — a reproductive-lag effect, not a direct response to abundance.
  • Events panel — trigger a drought, a bloom, or an instant die-off and watch the ribbon and census respond.

What's real

Every random draw is seeded. The same seed reproduces a run exactly. Where a hypothesis didn't pan out, the changelog below says so rather than dressing it up — a fluctuating-selection effect on traits was expected from seasons and didn't show up cleanly; the boom-bust population cycle that did show up wasn't the one predicted going in.
